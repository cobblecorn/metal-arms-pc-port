## Mining Laser controls, energy, and retail projectile behavior

Main files: `weapon_laser.cpp/.h`, `botglitch.cpp`, `botgrunt.cpp`,
`port/pc_laser_charge.h`, `port/pc_input.cpp/.h`, and `tracer.cpp/.h`.

- The common player path covers L2, L3, and possessed Mil lasers across missions
  and local player slots. L1 and NPC weapon input are unchanged.
- Mouse primary repeats ordinary shots. A short secondary/right-click tap uses
  the selected grenade/item on release. Holding past the original 0.75-second
  delay commits to charging. A blocked/canceled charge never becomes a grenade.
- Controllers retain retail analog primary behavior: partial pull charges;
  an initial full pull fires ordinary repeated shots. A charge already started
  remains held through a full squeeze until release. Secondary items remain
  immediate. Actual firing-device tracking is independent of prompt style and
  mouse aiming, supporting P1 keyboard/controller changes and separate P2 input.
- **Intentional custom rule:** every charged burst costs 25/100 energy for every
  player/input path. Four bursts are available from a full bar. Partial bursts
  cost the same. Original idle recharge continues. Retail instead resets the
  remaining energy to 25%; do not accidentally restore that behavior.
- Retail damage tiers begin at 50% and 95% charge. Original table values before
  armor: L2 20/35/50, L3 35/55/75, possessed Mil 50/75/100. The damage system
  converts these to normalized health-container units.
- Charged shots use original thick-projectile collision, surface reflection,
  wire pass-through, charge pitch, particles and decals. Launch metadata retains
  upgrade, damage tier, and damager if the weapon subsequently changes.
- Charged continuations defer their first work until the next simulation tick,
  preventing recursive same-tick reflection cascades. Ordinary shots and global
  effect/pool limits were not changed by this tracer flag.

Retail evidence is local `gamedata/sys/main.dol`, the original `w_laser.csv`
and `damage.csv`; addresses and detailed evidence are in
`docs/mining-laser-retail-parity-20261023.md`.

## Slosh and offset player reticles

`CBot::ComputeHumanTargetPoint_WS` in `bot.cpp` now converts horizontal reticle
offsets by viewport height/width before constructing targeting/assist rays.
Reticle drawing uses half-height units, while the former ray code treated X as
half-width units. Slosh's nonzero horizontal offset exposed this mismatch.
Centered Glitch targeting remains unchanged. NPC targeting, flame damage, muzzle
location and the shared muzzle-direction clamp are unchanged.

The earlier unsuccessful player-Slosh muzzle-clamp bypass was removed. Do not
reapply it. `sloshtank.cpp` raises the PC fuel-backpack pool from two instances
to MAX_PLAYERS plus one authored NPC; legacy capacity stays two.

## Added after the approved 1.10 candidate

### Level 15 booth arrival fallback

Files: `MAScriptTypes.cpp/.h`, `port/pc_booth_fallback.h`.
The earlier `_PortBoothEntranceGoal` repair remains intact.

Only campaign `WEWRresrch2`, during its cutscene, and the recognized original
`rsboothdoor.sma` bytecode layout qualify. The alarm must have started and the
script must be waiting for fewer than four guard arrivals. After 15 seconds of
simulation time, the fallback additionally waits for that scene's own camera
path and streaming speech to finish. It changes only the script's arrival count
and closing state. The retail script then closes the door, performs its original
one-second and half-second delays, releases the camera/control and saves the
checkpoint. No direct cutscene termination, guard teleport, or camera skip.
Late arrivals cannot restart the ending. Timers reset with scene/level state.

### Mil City Hub slope/doorway trap

Files: `bot.cpp`, `port/pc_city_slope.h`.
World: `WEMCcity_03` (level 24), near the last generator courtyard.

Reproduced in the real native level at approximately `(441,13.2,-817.3)`;
a second identical trap occurs near Z=-647.4. The player slides into opposing
contacts: the visible sloped exterior and an invisible, almost vertical static
terrain panel extending through it. The old build stayed at the same height,
including during a deliberately applied outward movement impulse.

The repair filters only those two captured terrain-panel fingerprints from
the bot movement solver while the controlled player's collision sphere is on
the exterior side of the actual roof plane (0.12 contact tolerance). It is
gated to this world and controlled non-vehicle bots. Interior-side wall contact,
visible roof geometry, floor contact, NPCs, vehicles, projectiles, and every
other map keep their original collision behavior. There is no teleport or
general noclip/unstick timer. Both the walkable and wall-contact selection loops
must apply the same exclusion. Numeric fingerprinting deliberately fails closed
if a different map revision changes those triangles.

The baseline native log and contact coordinates are in
`build/logs/slope-doorway-baseline.log` and `slope-doorway-contacts.log`.
Six descent starts passed after correction for solo and again for co-op P2;
they reached the floor before the fixture's later movement impulse began.

### Friendly/NPC Miner-model death breakup

Files: `ma/App/ma/botminer.cpp/.h`. Applies to CBotMiner (the old serialized
entity name is `BotBlink`), including `GRDMminer00`, `GRDMminer01` and
`GRDMminernl`. This is global to those model variants, in solo and co-op.

Three causes combined to remove these droids without a visible death:

- The part-manager creation code was disabled. Its old four-limb count also
  disagreed with the shipped `bp_Miner` table, which defines one head limb and
  nine bone breakup entries. Nonexistent arm-limb queries need bounds guards.
- `CBotMiner::InitSystem` reads `b_Miner` before localized debris/sound resources
  load. All five authored debris references were NULL. Refreshing the same
  data during bot creation resolves its original Miner groups and sound.
- The default death flags requested a missing death talk/animation entry and
  automatic persistence. The active part-manager path instead owns the visible
  death until its moving model pieces finish; stale checkpoint defaults are
  cleared on death too. Explicit script persistence remains respected.

The repair reuses the working shared `CBotPartMgr`/debris system with the
original Miner geometry, breakup table, debris groups, explosions and speeds.
Each mesh variant has its own pool because their bone indices differ. Pool
unregistration and reset use the existing shared lifetime handling. No damage,
health, armor, team, aggression or NPC-friendly-fire rule was changed. Existing
co-op damage protection is still limited to player-controlled victims; NPCs
retain the game's existing rules. This is source/data preservation, not a
separate claim that every retail platform's friendly-fire behavior was tested.

`tools/test_miner_death_native.py --players 1` and `--players 2` passed in the
real `WEDiinvas01` level: its authored friendly `droid2`, both alternate meshes,
player damage, a scripted death, airborne death, moving debris, eventual removal,
revival, another death and reset while debris is active. The 2P fixture also
checks player-to-player damage protection. Captured frames were reviewed and
show flying model pieces, sparks/fire and debris instead of instant removal.
These opt-in native checks use isolated saves; no ordinary full playthrough or
manual audio/retail visual-equivalence comparison is claimed.

## Open report — do not call this fixed

The user saw Rivet rounds pass through alarmed Mils after the level-15 booth,
then possessed-Titan MG fire pass through a Mil for 5–10 seconds later in Mil
City Hub. The copied live log records script events/deaths and exhausted impact
decal vertices; it does **not** record individual hit/miss or invulnerability
state. Decal exhaustion alone explains missing impact marks, not missing damage.
No global collision, friendly-fire, armor, or invulnerability change was made
from that inconclusive evidence. Source for the next investigation includes
`tracer.cpp`, the Titan weapon ray path, `CBot::HandleCollision`, mesh bounds /
animated collision updates, and cutscene invulnerability restoration. Capture
target identity, world, time, collision flags, hit tracker and damage rejection
at the actual occurrence. Do not infer a root cause from the decal warning.

Also outstanding: the exact lingering cabinet sound, a physical-controller
laser check, final tank MG listening balance, vehicle control feel, 4P Slosh
aiming, and an ordinary manual climb/descent of the pictured building.
